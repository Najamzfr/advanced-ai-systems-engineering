The assignment target is 100 requests/second with p95 below two seconds. Define whether two seconds means first token or complete response, plus prompt/output lengths, arrival pattern, model, precision, hardware, and quality threshold. A capacity proposal is not a claim that a local benchmark achieved the target.

Little's Law relates average concurrent work L to arrival rate λ and average time W in a stable system: L=λWL=\lambda W. It does not convert a p95 target directly into an average concurrency requirement or predict tail behavior under saturation. Estimate replicas from measured sustainable per-replica throughput, then allow headroom, failures, and load imbalance. Include queues, cold starts, fallback, and cost in the design.

Work through an example

If measured mean service residence is 0.8 seconds at 100 requests/second, average concurrency is approximately 80. If one replica sustains 25 requests/second at the required quality and latency, four replicas are a nominal estimate before headroom and failure tolerance. Neither calculation proves p95 compliance.

Read the explanation

Little's Law concerns averages in a stable system, not a tail guarantee. Service variability, queueing, length mix, scheduling, and overload determine p95; validate with representative load tests.

Cheat sheet

Capacity at 100 requests per second

Definition

An inference service admits requests, schedules model execution and returns generated tokens.

Intuition

Queueing, prefill and decoding contribute different parts of latency.

Formula

TTFT=tfirst token−tarrival\mathrm{TTFT}=t_{\mathrm{first\ token}}-t_{\mathrm{arrival}}

Use a declared request boundary. Queueing and prompt prefill contribute to time to first token.

Common mistake

Tokens per second alone does not establish the user's response-time target.

Production notes

Measure TTFT, completion latency, throughput, errors and memory under stated load.
