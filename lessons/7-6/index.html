A recovery drill begins with a known event and an expected terminal state. Inject one failure at a time, then inspect checkpoints, outbox rows, sink records, and spans. Confirm that completed work is reused, unsafe work is denied, and unknown effects remain visible. Measure recovery time and operator actions, not only whether the process restarts.

Use a local Compose stack or equivalent reproducible services. Seed fixtures, validate configuration, start dependencies, run a successful job, then repeat with duplicate event, unavailable MCP, database failure, model timeout, and crash-after-dispatch. Document dead-letter replay and rollback. Do not claim production reliability from one successful demonstration; the drill produces evidence for the Week 8 chaos matrix.

After recovery, no duplicate sends occur but the job remains pending forever. Has the drill passed?

Read the explanation

No. Safety without liveness is incomplete. Establish bounded recovery or explicit failed/unknown status, with alerting and a documented operator path.
