Run three architectures: single agent, planner, executor, and planner, researcher, verifier. Freeze cases, source access, model family, total tokens/calls, and timeout. Record actual consumed compute because a nominal step budget can hide retries or subagent calls. Compare both quality at a fixed budget and cost at a fixed quality threshold.

Use paired per-case differences and slices for direct questions, multi-source synthesis, contradictions, missing evidence, and attacks. Inspect whether planning improves task coverage, verification reduces unsupported claims, or added coordination merely increases latency. Retain failed runs and report uncertainty. Choose the least complex architecture meeting the project requirements; an inconclusive difference is a valid engineering result.

Work through an example

Architecture C scores 90% versus A at 87%, but consumes four times the tokens and has a wide confidence interval. The report should not call C superior without the matched-budget comparison and a justification for the operational cost.

Cheat sheet

Compare architectures under equal budgets

Definition

An evaluation compares versioned outputs with a task-specific definition of success.

Intuition

Measure the component that could cause the observed failure.

Formula

Recall@K⁡=∣relevant∩top K∣∣relevant∣\operatorname{Recall@K}=\frac{|\mathrm{relevant}\cap\mathrm{top}\ K|}{|\mathrm{relevant}|}

Declare the relevant set, retrieval unit and query denominator. Candidate recall is not answer accuracy.

Common mistake

A higher aggregate score can hide a regression on a critical slice.

Production notes

Freeze case IDs and report denominators, uncertainty and failed runs.
